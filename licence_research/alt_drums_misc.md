# Openly licensed replacements: drum machines, orchestra hit, harpsichord, VSCO upright

Researched 2026-11-20. Every licence below was read on the source's own page (Freesound sound page, Commons file page, GitHub LICENSE/README via the GitHub API). Quotes are as the page gives them. "Own unit" evidence is quoted where it exists.

Bottom line: **I found no acceptable real-hardware set for the TR-808 or the TR-909 that covers every voice.** The TR-606 and TR-707 have partial CC0 or CC BY candidates, but each comes from a modified or circuit-bent unit, so they need a listen first. Item 5 has one real-recording public-domain candidate that suits the purpose. Items 6a and 6b are clean.

---

## 1. Roland TR-808 (every voice)

**Nothing acceptable exists among sources I could find.** Freesound, Wikimedia Commons, archive.org (advancedsearch over all TR-808/909/707/606 items), Zenodo, GitHub (repo search plus specific repos) and musical-artifacts.com were all checked.

Near misses, ranked:

1. **Theofanis Siassios, "808patterns 01.ogg" (plus "808acctrig 01.ogg" and "808cbtrig 01.ogg")**, on Wikimedia Commons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:808patterns_01.ogg
   - Licence (file page): "This file is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 Unported license." The page also records VRT ticket #2017020810011321, which confirms the copyright holder approved the release. **This is CC BY-SA.**
   - Contents: "Recording of various patterns played by a TR-808 drum machine." The file is 53.5 s of Ogg Vorbis at about 396 kbps. The two other files are 21 s and 19 s of patterns that use the accent and cowbell trigger outputs.
   - Own-recording evidence: the author is credited as copyright holder through VRT, which implies he made the recording. The machine is not stated as his own.
   - Judgment: real 808 audio under a valid licence, but it is lossy and made of overlapping patterns. Use it at most to check a fit by ear, not as the fitting source.
2. **MckAudio/MckSamplePacks, folder TR8**: https://github.com/MckAudio/MckSamplePacks (licence CC0-1.0, LICENSE file "CC0 1.0 Universal")
   - Own-recording evidence: the README says "Samples of my drum machines… I made recordings of my drum machines / synthesizers", and TR8/README.md says "I recorded all one-shot sample sounds of the TR8 with a ZOOM U-24".
   - Contents: 48 kHz, 24-bit mono. Covers 808 BD 1-3 and Long 1-2; SD 1-4; low/mid/high toms in normal, short and long; conga low/mid/high in normal and short; rim; claves 1-2; clap and long clap; noise clap; snap; maracas; cowbell and long cowbell; crash in normal, short and long; CH and OH in several lengths. Also 909, 707, 606 and 727 voices.
   - **Caveat:** this is a Roland **TR-8**, a digital ACB model of the 808, not a real TR-808. It also falls under your "No Roland sounds" rule unless VA Roland counts as the exception.
   - Judgment: best licensed broad coverage, but it is a model of the target machine, not the target machine.
3. **Karman_Lyne, "Roland TR08 100% gain, default tuning"** (16 sounds): https://freesound.org/people/Karman_Lyne/packs/29226/
   - Licence: "Creative Commons 0".
   - Description: "Mono recording from a Roland TR08 into a MPC1000". 44.1 kHz, 16-bit.
   - Judgment: the TR-08 is the Boutique ACB model, so the same caveat as the TR-8 applies, and this set has fewer voices.

Rejected:
- **tidalcycles/sounds-tr808-fischer** (GitHub, CC0): a re-upload of Fischer's set. The CC0 was added by the repackager, not by Fischer.
- **Beckstrom's Weird TR-808 pack** (archive.org/details/808_variations, CC BY 4.0): the "original" folder gives no source for the dry hits, and nothing says they came from his own 808.
- **derickgtwk "TR-808 kick"** (CC0): "made in FL studio with roland tr 808 mixed with analog emulation plugins", so most likely a plugin.
- **iainmccurdy TR-808**: Csound synthesis, CC BY-NC.
- **A43's Drum Kit**: real 808 but processed, Sampling+ licence.
- **altemark LSDJ/Nord**: processed.
- **XHALE303**: patterns only, no licence visible without login.
- **Boochi44/free-drum-samples**: derived from the Fischer set.
- **WaivOps EDM-TR8** (Zenodo, CC BY): loops built from "custom drum samples" of unknown source.

## 2. Roland TR-909 (every voice)

**Nothing acceptable exists.**

Near misses:

1. **altemark, "TR-909 JGB pack"** (224 sounds): https://freesound.org/people/altemark/packs/1643/
   - Licence on the sound pages: "Attribution 4.0".
   - Provenance: "Sampled by Janne G:son Berg from his old 909. Cut up and organized by me." The quoted original readme says "Sampled in one session from my (now sold) 909. 24 bit, 44.1 kHz. Feel free to use the samples for whatever you like." Freesound files are 16-bit.
   - **Rejected under your rule.** It is a re-upload of someone else's set. The CC BY was chosen by the uploader, and the recordist's own terms are informal, just like Fischer's.
   - If Janne G:son Berg can be reached to confirm a CC licence, this becomes the best 909 source: real unit, 224 cuts including many snares, hats, ride and so on.
2. **MckAudio TR8** (above, CC0): includes 909 BD 1-3, AttackBD; SD 1-3, AttackSD; toms in three lengths; rim; clap and long clap; OH and CH, normal and short; crash; ride. ACB model, so the same caveat applies.
3. **Wikimedia Commons clips.** Both are short patterns that are only useful for listening checks:
   - "TR-909 sound example.ogg" by TBergman (Teemu Bergman), 16 s: "Own work", "licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 Unported license". https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TR-909_sound_example.ogg
   - "TR-909.ogg" by Sylvain87, 60 s house pattern, CC BY 1.0. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TR-909.ogg

Rejected:
- **Doctor Mix free 909**: "My own vintage Roland TR-909", 24-bit, 44.1 kHz, but no licence on the page.
- **GRD-music-**: JD-Xi, not a 909.
- **WaivOps EDM-TR9**: loops of unknown source.
- **BPB Cassette 909** and **Wave Alchemy 909 Tape**: EULA or no licence.
- **oramics**: catalogue re-hosts.

## 3. Roland TR-707 (every voice)

Partial candidates, ranked:

1. **hesed23, "7X7 Free Samples"** (15 sounds: BD1-2?, SD1, SD2, LT, MT, HT, RIM, COW, CP, Tamb, OHH, CR, Ride and others): https://freesound.org/people/hesed23/packs/3826/
   - Licence: "Attribution 4.0" (CC BY 4.0).
   - Own-unit evidence: each description reads "Free Sample from my modificated tr707". 44.1 kHz.
   - **Caveat:** the linked mod page (atm23.net/mod.php, archived 2011) shows "Roland Tr-707 Bending…", so the unit is **circuit-bent**. The same author has a second pack, "lockedgroove7x7" (11 sounds).
   - Judgment: the only full-kit, own-unit, CC BY 707 set I found. Listen before using; if the hits are bent, this fails.
2. **Emily Schmanks, "TR707 ROM cymbals compared to enveloped output.wav"**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TR707_ROM_cymbals_compared_to_enveloped_output.wav
   - Licence: "This file is made available under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication."
   - Own-unit evidence: "The other two were recorded by me personally using my Roland TR-707 as sound source." The other two sounds are ROM dumps supplied by Harry Axten.
   - Judgment: genuine and useful for the cymbal envelope only, 5.5 s in total.
3. **MckAudio TR8** (CC0): 707 BD 1-3, SD 1-3, toms in normal and short, rim, clap, tambourine, OH, CH, crash, ride. ACB model.

Rejected:
- **samplivor/TR-707** (GitHub): 30 files including accents, "I recorded all sounds", but the repo has no licence.
- **dyLAB** (matrixsynth, 2010): "I have resampled my TR-707", no licence.
- **BØLT 707clean** (itch.io): no licence.
- **yosualogan**: spring reverb, CC BY-NC.
- **drriquet**: CC0, but a loop with no ownership stated.

Note: the 707's voices are Roland's PCM ROM recordings, so fitting to a 707 is fitting to Roland samples. Check this against your "No Roland sounds" rule.

## 4. Roland TR-606 (every voice)

Partial candidates, ranked:

1. **o-toener, "Behringer RD-6 Analog Drum Machine"**: https://freesound.org/people/o-toener/packs/31348/
   - Licence: "Creative Commons 0".
   - Description: "Samples from my Behringer RD-6 SR analog drum machine, which is a clone of the legendary ROLAND TR-606. BD, SD, CLAP, CHH, OHH, CYMBAL, LT, HT." 24-bit, 44.1 kHz, normalized, recorded with a UMC404HD.
   - Judgment: an own-unit CC0 recording of an analog circuit clone, not a Roland. Every 606 voice is covered, and the RD-6 adds a clap.
2. **MckAudio RD6 folder** (same CC0 repo): 6 BD, 7 clap, 15 hats/cymbals, 12 SD, 12 toms. 48 kHz, 24-bit. Recorded by the repo owner ("Samples of my drum machines"). Same clone caveat. The repo's TR8 folder also has 606 BD, SD, toms, hats and cymbal.
3. **Real but modified TR-606 units** (CC0, need listening):
   - oceansonmars, "Modified 606" (40 one-shots): https://freesound.org/people/oceansonmars/packs/44238/. Description: "Sounds made using a Modified TR 606 Drum Machine". 24-bit, 44.1 kHz. Ownership and the nature of the mods are not stated.
   - vdb4f, "Beats" (39 loops): https://freesound.org/people/vdb4f/packs/9830/. Description: "Beats recorded from my modified Roland TR-606 analog drummachine". Tagged Circuit-Bend.

Rejected:
- **bdu "Roland TR606 sampleset"**: "from the fdiskc.com tr606 sample set", a re-upload with no licence visible.
- **Bauchamp "128 tr 606 tom"** (Commons, CC0): a single loop taken from the Free Music Archive.

## 5. Orchestra hit reference (GM 55), replacing TimGM6mb

1. **astro_denticle, "Public Domain Orchestra Hits"** (20 sounds, CC0): https://freesound.org/people/astro_denticle/packs/44108/
   - These are chopped from public-domain recordings, so cite the underlying source rather than the Freesound chop.
   - **a. "D#m Orch Hit -6 Infernal Dance"** (4 lengths): https://freesound.org/people/astro_denticle/sounds/820075/
     - Source: US Marine Band, *Infernal Dance* from Stravinsky's Firebird, the very passage the original Fairlight ORCH5 stab came from. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Infernal_Dance_from_The_Firebird_by_Stravinsky.ogg
     - Licence on Commons: PD-USGov-Military-Marines, "This file is a work of a United States Marine or employee, taken or made as part of that person's official duties." The original is on marineband.marines.mil.
     - **Caveat:** this is the Marine Band's wind-band arrangement (arranged by Thomas Knox), so there are **no strings**. It is also Vorbis at 110 kbps, and the Freesound chop is transposed down 6 semitones. Use the Commons original.
   - **b. "Eb Orch Hit Sym 5 … stab 03"**: Davis High School Symphony Orchestra via Musopen, described as "a CC0 recording". It is a real full orchestra with strings. Musopen's page returned 403, so I could not verify the licence at the source. Not verified.
   - The Berlioz and Janáček chops come from "European Archive" LP transfers. Their public-domain status is weak (EU recording term), so reject them.
2. **Marine Chamber Orchestra (US Marine Band), Beethoven Symphony No. 1, I.**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Symphony_No._1_in_C_-_I._Adagio_molto,_Allegro_con_brio_-_Chamber_Orchestra_-_United_States_Marine_Band.opus
   - Licence: PD-USGov-Military-Marines (quoted as above). The composition is public domain.
   - A real full orchestra with strings, performed 27 Jan 2019. The Allegro con brio has tutti stabs.
   - Lossy: Opus at 141 kbps, sourced from YouTube.
   - Judgment: the government is the recordist, so this is the cleanest real tutti-with-strings source. Chop a tutti chord yourself.
3. **Fallback for a GM-set hit sample: GeneralUser GS v2.0.3**, licence text at documentation/LICENSE.txt in github.com/mrbumpy409/GeneralUser-GS.
   - The licence says "Please feel free to use it in your software projects". It also says "I cannot be 100% sure where all of the samples originated".
   - This is a custom licence, not CC, and sample provenance is unknown. It is already your level reference.

Rejected:
- **MathewHenry "Orchestral Hits 1-3"** (CC0, dry, 13 instrumentations): the description never says how they were made. They are almost certainly a sample-library render.
- **BigDumbWeirdo "Orch Hit"** (CC0): "made from instrument samples recorded under studio conditions", source unknown.
- **FreePats GM**: GPL-3.

## 6a. Harpsichord other than VCSL (replacing Ross)

1. **pjcohen, "Harpsichord"** (60 sounds, CC0): https://freesound.org/people/pjcohen/packs/21464/
   - Sample page https://freesound.org/people/pjcohen/sounds/381515/ shows licence "Creative Commons 0".
   - Description: "Harpsichord recorded with overhead mics". 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo.
   - Files are named Harpsichord_<note>.wav and run roughly D1 to G#5. 60 files suggests five chromatic octaves, one take per note.
   - Maker, model and registration are not stated. pjcohen has 781 instrument recordings (upright bass, tambourines, cymbals), which fits a first-person recordist, though the page does not say "my".
   - Judgment: the best openly licensed alternative I found.

Rejected:
- **loofa "Clavecin"** (two U87s, note by note): CC BY-NC.
- **sfzinstruments/Clavecin**: "Converted from the original GigaSampler version by S. Christian Collins", no licence.
- **sverweij/pluck-sfz**: synthetic.
- **Katalyst CC0 harpsichord**: built from VCSL.
- **mogigrumbles "CS-80 HARPSICHORD"**: a Yamaha CS-80 patch, not a harpsichord.

## 6b. VSCO 2 CE Upright Piano: is it explicitly CC0?

**Yes, at the repository level, and the folder note is consistent with that.** Sources: repo https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE (GitHub-detected licence CC0-1.0).

- **LICENSE** begins "CC0 1.0 Universal / Statement of Purpose…"
- **Keys/Upright Piano/Info.txt** (committed 2016-07-08), quoted in full: "Upright Piano sampled by Simon Dalzell of Ivy Audio, Winter 2015-16. Bearer is granted right to redistribute this sample set by Versilian Studios LLC. Credit to both original author and Versilian Studios is encouraged."
- **Readme.txt (Terms)**: "You are permitted to use these samples for ANY purpose. We ask that you do not sell the samples directly, and encourage you to keep any work done on improving the sample set open and public. Please provide credit to Versilian Studios/Sam Gossner, and/or Ivy Audio/Simon Dalzell where applicable".
  - "We ask" is a request, not a condition, and shipping fitted numbers is not selling samples.
- **Homepage** (versilian-studios.com/vsco-community): "The library is under a Creative Commons 0 (i.e. public domain) license."
- **README.md**: "Recorded by Sam Gossner & Simon Dalzell".
- Contents: 69 wav files in Keys/Upright Piano.
- Separately, Keys/Upright Nr1 (Versilian's own "VS Upright Nr. 1", pp/mf/f with round-robins) has the note "These are the direct samples from VS Upright Nr. 1- feel free to edit or refine to taste."
- Judgment: the Upright Piano sits inside a repository published under CC0, with Versilian stating it holds redistribution rights from Dalzell. Dalzell himself has not written a CC0 statement anywhere I found, so this is CC0 through Versilian's grant. If that matters, Upright Nr1 is Versilian's own recording and has no third-party question.
